<p>
  Paul is a computational cognitive neuroscientist who develops formal models of learning and planning, and of how they go awry in
  psychopathology. Because planning is powerful but costly, much of his work asks how people plan efficiently: when to plan and when to put
  it off, whether to plan forward from where they are or backward from their goals, and how much of a task to represent in the first place.
  He also studies offline learning, in which people use memories and a model of the world to simulate actions and their outcomes between
  decisions or during rest. In psychopathology, Paul proposes that anxiety distorts people's internal models, or construals, of the tasks
  they face, leading to excessive planning, re-checking of decisions already made, and poor generalization. Paul is excited to work with
  aspiring trainees at any level with psychology, neuroscience, and computer science backgrounds. His work has been published in top outlets
  such as Nature Human Behaviour, Trends in Cognitive Sciences, and Psychological Science.
</p>
