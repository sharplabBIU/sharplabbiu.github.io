### Our lab uses [computational models](https://doi.org/10.1177/0963721418818441) to explain human learning, planning and how they are altered in [psychopathology](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/tops.70051). Located in Bar-Ilan's [brain center](https://gondabrain.biu.ac.il/en), we're investigating how trait anxiety biases planning [content](https://osf.io/preprints/psyarxiv/98ktg_v4), [meta-control](https://osf.io/preprints/psyarxiv/8ubct_v1), and [efficiency](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-024-01930-8). To do so we use [online tasks](https://www.prolific.com/), advanced [virtual reality](https://somniumspace.com/somniumvr1), [eye-tracking](https://www.sr-research.com/solutions/fmri-meg-solutions/), and [MEG](https://meglabbiu.org/).

### Our recent work models meta-control over [when](https://osf.io/preprints/psyarxiv/8ubct_v1) and [how](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-024-01930-8) to plan, and how worriers [remove actions correlated with but not causing threat](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/tops.70051) from task representations used to plan. These studies are motivated by our [theoretical account of worry](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1364661324002924) as arising from biased threat-avoidant planning. Ongoing work treats anxiety as a property of a [person’s world model](https://osf.io/preprints/psyarxiv/98ktg_v4), which comprises domain-specific beliefs about threat, control and the self, and we are in the process of designing personalized tasks to measure how those beliefs shape task representations used to learn and plan in biased ways.
