### Our lab uses [computational models](https://doi.org/10.1177/0963721418818441) to explain human learning, planning and how they are altered in [psychopathology](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/tops.70051). Located in Bar-Ilan's [brain center](https://gondabrain.biu.ac.il/en), we're investigating how trait anxiety biases planning [content](https://osf.io/preprints/psyarxiv/98ktg_v4), [meta-control](https://osf.io/preprints/psyarxiv/8ubct_v1), and [efficiency](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-024-01930-8). To do so we use [online tasks](https://www.prolific.com/), advanced [virtual reality](https://somniumspace.com/somniumvr1), [eye-tracking](https://www.sr-research.com/solutions/fmri-meg-solutions/), and [MEG](https://meglabbiu.org/).

### Our recent work models how people adaptively decide [when](https://osf.io/preprints/psyarxiv/8ubct_v1) and [how](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-024-01930-8) to plan, and that worriers engage biased planning by [removing policies correlated with but not causing threat](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/tops.70051). These studies, originally motivated by our account of [altered planning in anxiety](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1364661324002924), have since been updated by our [world-model theory of trait anxiety](https://osf.io/preprints/psyarxiv/98ktg_v4). We treat anxiety as a property of a person’s world model, which comprises domain-specific beliefs about threat, control and the self, and are in the process of designing personalized tasks to measure how those beliefs shape task representations used to learn and plan in biased ways.
